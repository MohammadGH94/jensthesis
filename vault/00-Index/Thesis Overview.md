# Thesis Overview

**Title:** Plasma Biomarkers of Neurodegeneration Across the Spectrum of Brain Health
**Author:** [[Jennifer Cooper]]
**Degree:** Doctor of Philosophy, Pathology and Laboratory Medicine, UBC

## Abstract (paraphrased)

Plasma biomarkers — particularly for Alzheimer's diagnosis — have advanced rapidly in the past decade and offer accessible, low-cost, minimally invasive tools for early detection. But significant gaps remain before they can be implemented clinically in Canada. This thesis addresses several of those gaps through analysis of [[Aβ42-40]], [[p-tau-181]], [[NfL]], and [[GFAP]] across four cohorts spanning the spectrum of brain health.

## Research hypothesis

Plasma biomarkers of neurodegeneration can be standardised, interpreted against age-specific norms, validated against autopsy-confirmed neuropathology, and translated into clinical practice in Canada.

## Specific objectives

1. Establish age-specific [[Reference Curve Modelling|reference intervals and curves]] in a normative Canadian population ([[CHMS]]).
2. Characterise biomarker profiles in [[Cognitive Resilience|cognitively resilient]] elderly ([[Super Seniors]]).
3. Examine the modifying effect of [[APOE4]] genotype on biomarker concentrations.
4. Link plasma biomarkers to [[ADNC|autopsy-confirmed AD neuropathology]] ([[CARD]]).
5. Explore biomarkers for detecting [[Co-pathologies]] (α-syn, [[TDP-43 Pathology|TDP-43]], CVD).
6. Validate [[Plasma Probability Scores]] for AD prevalence in [[COMPASS-ND]].
7. Frame results for [[Clinical Implementation in Canada]].
